# Further sources — ranked by effect on tool quality

Not a reading list. Each entry says what it would change in the product.

## 1. The draft implementing regulation and its 122 feedback submissions

Consultation ran 13 March – 18 April 2025, Have Your Say initiative **14449**, **122
submissions**.

Why it matters for the tool, not for any one case:

- **An ambiguity map across all 26 categories.** 122 industry submissions, written by people
  with real products, saying which descriptions are unclear. That is WP5's edge-case input
  handed over ready-made, and it is evidence rather than our guesses about which categories
  are hard.
- **It already surfaced a core engine question.** A coalition submission argued the
  core-functionality test lets one product fall under several categories, and asked that each
  product be assigned to one category only. The Commission did not adopt that. So
  **multi-category matches are a real, expected outcome** — which means our conflict handling
  is a central feature, not an edge case.
- **Travaux** for any category whose wording changed between draft and final.

One download, three payoffs. Highest value on this list.

## 2. The "Blue Guide" on the implementation of EU product rules

The canonical cross-sectoral source for **placing on the market**, **making available**,
**substantial modification**, and the manufacturer / importer / distributor / own-brand
roles.

The CRA uses these as terms of art inherited from the New Legislative Framework rather than
defining them from scratch. They govern our entire `LIFE-*` and `ROL-*` families — 12 of 72
cases — and we are currently interpreting them from the CRA text alone.

This is the one gap most likely to produce confidently wrong answers across many products.

## 3. Second opinions from people

- **ORC WG CRA Hub** — open working group, public issue tracker, practitioners. Good place
  to post interpretation questions; answers are public and citable as expert interpretation
  (level 3).
- **National market surveillance authorities** — Belgium (FPS Economy), Netherlands (RDI);
  several Member States run CRA helpdesks. They will be enforcing this. A written answer is
  worth more than any commentary.
- **Notified bodies and test houses** — TÜV, Nemko and others publish classification
  guidance. Low authority, useful as a check on whether our reading is eccentric.

## 4. Adjacent regimes that change what users must do

- **RED Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30 and the EN 18031 series** — already impose
  cybersecurity requirements on radio equipment. Many in-scope products are radio equipment,
  so this affects what manufacturers have already done and bears on Art. 2(5) overlap.
- **NIS 2 and the CER Directive** — not for classification, but needed to explain why
  critical-infrastructure status does not change a product class.

## 5. Travaux of the CRA itself

COM(2022) 454, procedure **2022/0272(COD)**, plus Council and Parliament positions. Worth
consulting per-category when a description's wording is genuinely unclear — not as a
systematic exercise.

## Suggested order

1. The consultation feedback — ambiguity map plus travaux
2. The Blue Guide — unblocks the timeline and role families
3. Post open questions to ORC WG, since answers take time to arrive
4. Everything else on demand
